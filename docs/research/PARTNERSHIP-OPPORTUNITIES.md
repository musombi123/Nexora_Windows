# NEXORA partnership opportunities and pilot framework

**Prepared:** 25 November 2026  
**Use:** Partnership discussions, collaborator evaluation and founder preparation.

## 1. Partnership thesis

NEXORA is building a local-first game production workflow around structured project data, procedural generation, Blender asset creation, Godot assembly and a paired desktop agent. The strongest collaborators are those who can improve a measurable part of that workflow or validate whether it solves a real creator problem.

Avoid pursuing partnerships solely for logo placement or broad announcements. Agree on a deliverable that can be demonstrated.

## 2. Priority work packages

| Work package | Suitable partner | Pilot output | Evidence of success |
|---|---|---|---|
| Godot pipeline reliability | Godot developers / technical studios | One reference project that assembles, opens, runs and exports consistently | Reproducible build log, test matrix, defect list |
| 3D asset and animation quality | Technical artists / 3D studios | Reviewed character, prop or environment asset pack with metadata | Godot import, rig/animation check, asset provenance |
| Creator usability | Game-design educators / creator communities | Beginner completes one project using the guided workflow | Task completion, time-to-first-playable, feedback |
| Local device compatibility | Hardware labs / QA groups | Hardware and toolchain acceptance matrix | Repeated measurements across declared device profiles |
| Rights-safe asset workflow | Asset libraries / legal-IP specialists | Audited asset provenance and creator-consent process | Licence register, consent flow, deletion test |
| Export and publishing workflows | Publishers / distribution specialists | A draft handoff and preflight review for one supported target | Correct manifest, package validation, explicit publisher approval |
| Applied research | Universities / labs | Research pilot on reproducible procedural generation or local compute | Protocol, code, dataset rights, repeatable results |

## 3. Partner profiles

### Technical development partner
A team experienced in Godot, Blender, Python, desktop agents, build systems or game asset pipelines. The engagement should name specific modules, acceptance criteria, test fixtures and ownership of resulting code.

### Creative production partner
A game designer, artist, animator, audio producer or small studio that can help judge whether generated project outputs are useful and coherent. A partner should be able to supply original or licensed materials and grant documented testing/portfolio rights.

### Education and training partner
A school, college, bootcamp, makerspace or educator able to evaluate beginner onboarding and teachability. Pilot success should be measured by completed creator tasks and learner feedback, not attendance alone.

### QA and device partner
A testing group that can execute clean installs, run generated games, validate input/control behavior and report repeatable performance on declared hardware. A CI export is not a substitute for this acceptance.

### Distribution or publishing partner
A partner familiar with platform requirements, content review, creator monetization or game distribution. Publisher account ownership and final submission approval remain with the creator unless a separate agreement expressly provides otherwise.

### Strategic or funding partner
A partner interested in enabling specific product milestones. Funding requests should follow a scoped budget, milestone plan, use-of-funds statement and measurable acceptance criteria.

## 4. Pilot structure

A practical first pilot should be small enough to finish and review:

**Step 1 — Problem definition**
Select a creator segment and one workflow: for example, a beginner builds a small 3D exploration prototype from an authored brief.

**Step 2 — Success criteria**
Define what must work: project creation, local pairing, generation, scene assembly, Godot opening, gameplay smoke test, export and creator review.

**Step 3 — Rights and data**
Use synthetic or rights-cleared portraits/media/assets. Record licence, consent, provenance, deletion and publication rights.

**Step 4 — Responsibilities**
Name the NEXORA owner, partner owner, technical reviewer, deliverables, meeting cadence, test environment and escalation route.

**Step 5 — Commercial and IP terms**
Agree whether the work is paid, grant-funded, sponsored, reciprocal or exploratory. Define ownership of pre-existing IP, new deliverables, reusable code, assets, portfolio/showcase rights and confidentiality.

**Step 6 — Acceptance and next decision**
Publish a factual test report. Continue only if the workflow improves against the original baseline and both sides agree on the next scope.

## 5. Partner evaluation questions

Before entering an agreement, ask:

1. What user segment or production problem can this partner help us understand?
2. What exact deliverable can they commit to?
3. What evidence will show that the deliverable works?
4. Who owns code, designs, models, music and documentation produced?
5. Which third-party licences and redistribution conditions apply?
6. What data will be shared, where will it be stored and who can access it?
7. What are the costs, fees, payment milestones and responsibilities?
8. Can either party publish the work or use the other's brand?
9. What happens if the pilot fails or is discontinued?
10. What is the smallest next commitment?

## 6. Red flags

- A partner asks for unrestricted control of all repositories or credentials.
- A contributor expects ownership terms to be decided after delivering work.
- A partner proposes using copyrighted source material without rights or consent.
- A publisher promises release acceptance or guaranteed sales.
- A funding conversation requires unsupported user counts, valuation or revenue claims.
- A partner asks for personal creator assets or customer data without a clear purpose, consent and deletion process.

## 7. First meeting pack

Bring:

- the partnership overview;
- a short live demonstration using a small synthetic project;
- the architecture diagram and local/cloud boundary;
- a current feature/readiness matrix;
- one proposed pilot brief;
- an IP/licence and confidentiality discussion outline;
- a realistic milestone and cost estimate.

Do not hand over secrets, production data or the entire private IP portfolio at an introductory meeting.
